# ARDS — repository instructions

ARDS (Algorithmic Reasoning Development System) is a local algorithm-practice, correctness-proof, and learner-assessment workspace. Favor evidence, mathematical precision, and preservation of the learner's independent work.

## Project invariants

- **Source of truth:** `data/ards.db` is the canonical challenge, draw, attempt, assessment, and exposure archive. Inspect current state before reporting it. JSON, CSV, archived benchmarks, and old Markdown summaries are not live status.
- **Clean baseline:** On 2026-10-21 the learner authorized another full active-state reset **without creating any backup**, discarding 10 active challenges (8 released), their reviewer files, one queued batch and two reveal records. `data/ards.db` and `data/ards-studio.db` were initialized with empty schemas. Older external archived histories remain outside the active database and must **never** be mistaken for current progress, automatically restored, or silently regenerated. Do not import or resurrect old challenges. New Classic RACG draws begin at RACG-0001; preserve subsequent real draws, attempts, assessments and versioned correction history. Use the preview-first `scripts/reset_ards.py --confirm-reset --no-backup` command only when the learner explicitly requests another unbacked reset, with the website stopped.
- **Additional learner-requested batch (2026-10-21):** The original 110 RACG items consist of the first 10 and Batch 2 (RACG-0011–0110), all released. The requested additional 20 are separate Batch 3 (RACG-0111–0130); on 2026-10-21 all 20 were released through `ards_review.check` and `ards2.release` after 185 differential cases per item (2 official samples, 3 edge cases, 180 generated), matching source-quoted PPC reviews and immutable verification records. The review identity explicitly discloses that candidate and oracle were separately implemented and reviewed **in the same AI session as authoring**, not by independent personnel, nor formally certified. Their private teacher PPCs remain subject to the normal reveal gate. `python3 scripts/ards_batch.py status --batch 3` is authoritative. Student-only preview and provenance files are under `data/authoring/batch3_*`; all 20 are now visible on the released website. The older 110 were never reauthored by this additional-batch task. Do not regenerate or reroll these completed selections.
- **Default generator:** Classic RACG v1.2 (Randomized Algorithmic Challenge Generator), with six weighted difficulty levels (30%, 25%, 20%, 12%, 8%, 5% from Level 1 to 6), 15% topic continuation, eligible-tail renormalization on continuation instead of clamping, and no mastery gate. Use the exact recorded Y bounds and mapping in `references/racg.md`. Never invent rolls, reroll inconvenient topics, or treat stress fixtures as real practice problems. **For requests for N problems, use the durable batch queue in `scripts/ards_batch.py` and read `references/batch_authoring.md`. A queued selection is not an authored problem; author all N with private P1/P2/C/K, independently track verification, and never stop the batch merely because one family cannot be verified.**
- **Independent assessment:** The new `scripts/ppc_assess.py` records evidence-labeled reports from separate AI/human reviewers but does not autonomously prove a PPC correct. Follow `references/ppc_assessment_workflow.md` and preserve undetermined conclusions. Keep the teacher's full PPC private until explicitly revealed. Record answer exposure before disclosure. Subsequent attempts are practice-only; a correctly assessed, independent first attempt *already passed before reveal* retains credit. A submitted draft is not automatically graded.
- **Release integrity:** Database-resident authoring-batch membership enforces plain-text stdin/stdout and independent-review checks; an author-supplied `batch_id` and first-party test success never bypass these checks. The independent review evidence binds the current runner, report and code hashes. For repeated resets use preview-first `scripts/reset_ards.py`, after stopping the website.
- **Proof assurance:** Distinguish a proposed proof, structural check, bounded testing, an independent AI critique, restricted machine check, and theorem-kernel validation. Never promote one into another or claim arbitrary Java/algorithm correctness from a limited proof fragment. Before releasing a generated batch problem, follow `references/independent_review.md`: independent executable candidate and oracle, edge/random/sample checks, substantive written report, immutable file-bound evidence.
- **Difficulty-sensitive comprehension (mandatory):** Before authoring/reviewing new student-facing problems read **both** `references/contest_problem_design.md` (CodeChef collection lessons, required design sequence, and RACG-0040 full ACCEPT/REJECT example) **and** the six operational profiles, worked accept/reject examples, and reviewer pass tests in `references/specification_comprehension.md` for the exact randomized level. Do not settle for generic statements that a task is 'beginner-friendly' or 'abstract'; explicitly compare the learner-facing words to that level's examples. At **Levels 1–3**, the task's objects, relationships, and required output must be understandable without prior study of the algorithmic topic: explain unfamiliar terms such as undirected, connectivity, or other specialized domain concepts in plain language, and introduce mathematical variables before their use becomes confusing. At **Levels 4–6**, interpreting specialized terminology or an abstract formal model can itself be part of the challenge; do not oversimplify it. **At every level**, the statement must remain objectively precise, self-consistent, and fully evaluable; never create artificial difficulty through missing information or ambiguity. Keep the intended algorithm/proof private.
- **Student-facing statements:** Draft the problem's meaningful rules and **reasoning objective before the encoding**: initial objects, actions, state changes, order, required answer, exceptional cases; then Input Format, Output Format, Constraints and verified Samples. The main description alone must give an appropriate reader enough to understand and judge the task **without decoding command codes or knowing the intended solution**. Follow the RACG-0040 accepted-versus-rejected model in `references/contest_problem_design.md`; never accept terse update/query syntax as a substitute for explaining the operation. Honor real story/direct draws, genuine reasoning diversity, and nonspoiling samples; avoid meaningless stories, algorithm names/hints, accidental task reskins, and artificial padding tokens in NEW problems. For competition tasks use standard CodeChef-like stdin/stdout tokens and lines, **never JSON-only batch inputs**. Newly registered and released batch packages must pass the contest I/O gate; historical JSON drafts require per-problem versioned conversion with independent review, not cosmetic formatting. Reference PPCs follow `references/ppc_authoring_style.md`, including its **complete Level 1 P1/C worked example**: P1 specifies the full mathematical input domain and output relation (plus applicable boundaries/feasibility/optimization), and C makes a universal claim about the actual P2 and justifies **each P1 obligation**, including termination and boundary cases. A one-line output slogan or unsupported proof paragraph is not sufficient, even for Level 1. **Teacher PPC presentation is mandatory:** structure all four sections as readable Markdown, use `\(...\)` for inline mathematics and `\[...\]` for prominently displayed equations (rather than raw ASCII symbols), and separate theorem/claim, proof reasoning, and termination into appropriate paragraphs or proof-method-specific headings. Review rendered math and formatting before release; older plain-text references receive only conservative display-only improvements and require versioned revision for a fully reauthored proof. Use suitable proof techniques rather than obligatory invariants. Queued releases additionally require the independent, source-quoted P1-to-C `ppc_review` gate in `references/independent_review.md`; test passes and review flags alone are not a mathematical proof. Student statements contain no sample Explanation or Note sections; put every needed rule in the description, input/output contract, or constraints. Keep solutions and solution-derived hints out of statements, previews, and public endpoints. Respect the saved RACG story/direct draw. **Unreleased batch drafts are private to the authoring/review CLI and must not enter the learner-facing problem library.** Only individually released, evidence-backed problems become independently assessable.
- **Report observed results:** Do not claim edits, tests, verification, installs, imports, or persistent saves unless the corresponding operation actually succeeded.

## Use task-specific instructions, not every document

Codex skills live in `.agents/skills/<name>/SKILL.md`. Load the relevant skill only when the task calls for it:

- `classic-racg` — generate, verify, publish, or revise a randomized challenge.
- `ppc-verification` — formalize a PPC, inspect a proof, or run formal/Lean checks.
- `learner-assessment` — record attempts, evaluate performance, or handle solution exposure.
- `ards-website` — change the ARDS website, statement rendering, UI, or public API.

Consult `README.md` for commands and architecture; `references/` for authoritative mathematical specifications; and `website/PROBLEM_AUTHORING_STANDARD.md` for public statement formatting. Read these **when relevant**, not before every small edit.

## Working approach

Make focused changes; inspect affected code and data contracts. The local test suite uses disposable fixtures and may be run without repeated approval. Verify the behavior you changed, run broader tests when warranted, and resolve relevant regressions before reporting completion. Avoid touching the canonical learner archive merely to test or improve UI.

Continue through implementation, appropriate checks, and documentation updates for the requested scope. Ask before destructive history rewrites or exposing protected teacher material; do not require approval for safe local inspection and isolated tests.
